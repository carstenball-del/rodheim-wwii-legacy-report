## 2. Mission-count conflict (do not paper over)

| Source | Combat missions |
|---|---|
| Form 100 (12 Mar 1946 interview + records) | **2 missions, 18 combat hours** |
| 1945 newspaper clipping (family file) | **26 missions** |
| Air Medal ribbon in museum photos | oak-leaf clusters implying more than two sorties if 8th AF 5-mission scale is used |

Eighteen combat hours is a correct duration for **two** Chelveston–Saarland B-17 sorties. It cannot be the hour total for 26 credited missions.

Do not let Form 100 silently overwrite the clipping. Do not let the clipping silently overwrite Form 100. Awards boards need 366th loading lists, morning reports, Form 5, and Air Medal orders.

## 3. MACR rule (Issue #3)

Use **MACR 4874** for Rodheim / Stefaniak / 11 May 1944 / B-17 42-39966.

Do **not** treat **MACR 7702** as Rodheim's loss. 7702 is a later loss of the **same aircraft serial** (3 August 1944), different mission, different crew. Same tail number is how the two files get confused. Family holdings of 7702 are useful aircraft history only.

Canonical loss identifiers:

- MACR 4874 — NARA Catalog NAID 90970052, RG 92, M1380 fiche 01740
- German captured file KU-763A — NAID 142686000 (file unit titled "Rodheim, Seymour")

- 8th Air Force Mission 351, 11 May 1944, Saarbrücken / Saar-Mosel yards (Merkwiller also appears in some catalogs as the assigned target area)

## 4. Antisubmarine campaign — negative finding

Form 100 names only "8th Air Force based in England." No Antisubmarine Squadron, no AAFAC, no I Bomber Command.

WDGO 85 (10 Oct 1945) § IV lists the units entitled to Antisubmarine credit. 305th BG / 366th BS are not on that list. They are credited under Air Offensive, Europe and later EAME campaigns.

Labrador / Iceland / Canada / Ireland / Scotland on this form are the North Atlantic ferry pipeline, not American Theater ocean ASW patrols. Do not combine ASW sorties with 305th/366th missions. Do not claim a bronze service star on the American Campaign Medal for Antisubmarine from 305th service alone. The basic American Campaign Medal may still be proper from CONUS training time and/or the ocean movement.
